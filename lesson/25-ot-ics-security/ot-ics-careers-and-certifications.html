OT/ICS Careers & Certifications

You've now covered the technical foundation of OT/ICS security end to end. This closing lesson answers the practical question: how does someone actually build a career in this specific, high-demand field?

:::tip OT/ICS security remains one of the most talent-starved specializations in the entire security industry — the skill combination it requires (security fundamentals plus real comfort with physical, industrial systems) is genuinely rare, which is exactly why this path exists in this course at all. :::

Why demand outpaces supply here

Most cybersecurity professionals come from an IT background and have never worked with a PLC, a historian, or a Purdue Model architecture in practice. Most industrial engineers and operations staff have deep process knowledge but limited security training. The candidates who can speak both languages — security concepts and the physical-process constraints this entire path has emphasized (availability and safety over confidentiality, why patching isn't the first tool reached for) — are genuinely scarce, and that scarcity translates directly into strong compensation and career leverage for people who build this specific skill set.

Common entry paths into OT/ICS security

  • From IT security, learning OT — the most common path: a security analyst or engineer with solid IT fundamentals who deliberately builds OT-specific knowledge (this path, plus hands-on protocol and Purdue Model familiarity) to specialize
  • From operations/engineering, learning security — someone with real hands-on plant, SCADA, or process-engineering experience who adds security fundamentals (much of Paths 10-12 of this course) on top of existing physical-systems knowledge — often an even stronger candidate, because the process expertise is harder to fast-track than the security fundamentals are
  • Direct entry through vendor or integrator roles — starting at an ICS equipment vendor or a system integrator (the same role type covered in the supply chain lesson) provides deep hands-on exposure to real industrial equipment, a strong stepping stone into a dedicated OT security role afterward

Relevant certifications

  • GICSP (Global Industrial Cyber Security Professional) — the most widely recognized OT/ICS-specific certification, covering exactly the material this path builds toward: ICS architecture, protocols, and security controls
  • CISSP with an ICS/OT specialization focus — a general security credential (covered in Path 20's certification roadmap) that carries weight when paired with demonstrable OT-specific experience
  • Vendor-specific certifications — from major ICS/SCADA vendors (Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider Electric, and others), valuable for roles concentrated on that vendor's specific equipment ecosystem

Building demonstrable OT/ICS experience without plant access

Most newcomers don't have access to a real industrial facility to practice on. Realistic ways to build credible, demonstrable experience anyway:

  • Home-lab Modbus/DNP3 simulation — open-source ICS simulators let you practice protocol analysis and attack/defense concepts covered earlier in this path without any real physical equipment
  • CTF challenges with an ICS/OT theme — competitions increasingly include OT-flavored challenges specifically because the field is in demand; solving and writing them up (Path 19's CTF write-up template applies directly here) is genuine, portfolio-worthy evidence of hands-on understanding
  • Contributing to or studying public ICS incident writeups — the real-world attacks lesson's case studies (Stuxnet, the Ukraine grid attacks, Oldsmar, Triton/Trisis) are extensively documented publicly; being able to explain the technical mechanics of each one in an interview is a genuine differentiator

No single item alone closes the gap — the combination of study, hands-on practice, and a credible certification target is what makes a six-month IT-to-OT transition realistic.

Key terms

  • GICSP — Global Industrial Cyber Security Professional, the most widely recognized OT/ICS-specific security certification
  • System integrator role — a common entry point into hands-on ICS exposure, deploying and configuring real industrial equipment for clients

Recap

OT/ICS security remains talent-starved precisely because it demands a rare combination — real security fundamentals paired with genuine comfort around physical, industrial systems and their availability/safety priorities — that most IT security professionals and most operations engineers each only have half of. Building a credible path in requires deliberate effort on whichever half is missing: this path's technical foundation, hands-on lab practice, and a recognized certification like GICSP are the concrete, demonstrable pieces that close that gap.
