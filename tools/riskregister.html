Risk Register Builder

Add risks with likelihood and impact, get an auto-scored severity, and export the register as Markdown.

A risk register is how security work gets funded. It records what could go wrong, how likely and how damaging that is, and — the part people skip — what you have decided to do about it and who owns that decision.

How to use it

  1. Describe the risk.
  2. Score likelihood and impact from 1 to 5.
  3. Choose a treatment: Mitigate, Accept, Transfer or Avoid.
  4. Add to the register and export as Markdown.

What to take away

Score is likelihood times impact

A 5×5 grid gives 1 to 25. The number is only there to sort the list — do not defend it to two decimal places.

Accept is a real answer

Not every risk should be fixed. Accepting one explicitly, with a named owner and a review date, is a legitimate and auditable decision. Ignoring one is not.

Transfer does not move the consequences

Insurance and outsourcing move financial exposure. Your customers and your regulator still hold you responsible for the breach.

Frequently asked questions

What are the four risk treatment options?

Mitigate (reduce it), Accept (live with it knowingly), Transfer (share the financial impact), and Avoid (stop doing the risky thing). Every risk gets exactly one, chosen deliberately.

How often should a register be reviewed?

Quarterly for most organisations, and immediately after any significant change or incident. A register nobody revisits is documentation, not management.

What is residual risk?

What remains after your controls are in place. It is the number that should be accepted or escalated — the pre-control score is only useful for showing the controls are worth their cost.
