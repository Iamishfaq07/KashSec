MITRE ATT&CK Mapper

A searchable reference of real ATT&CK tactics and techniques, tied back to where this course covers each one.

MITRE ATT&CK is the shared map of how intrusions actually unfold — tactics describing what an attacker is trying to achieve, and techniques describing how. It is the vocabulary detection engineers, threat hunters and red teams use to talk to each other precisely.

How to use it

  1. Search by technique name, tactic, or ATT&CK ID.
  2. Read the matching entries and the tactic each belongs to.

What to take away

Tactic is the why, technique is the how

Credential Access is a tactic. Kerberoasting is a technique serving it. Reports that name only the technique leave out the intent.

IDs are the stable reference

Names get revised; T-numbers do not. Cite the ID in detections and reports so they survive renaming.

Coverage maps expose blind spots

Plotting your detections onto the matrix shows which tactics you cannot see at all — usually a more useful exercise than counting how many rules you have.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between ATT&CK and the Cyber Kill Chain?

The Kill Chain is a short linear model of an intrusion. ATT&CK is a far more granular catalogue of observed behaviours, and it does not assume attackers move in a straight line — because they do not.

How is ATT&CK actually used day to day?

To tag detections, to structure red team plans and reports, to compare tooling coverage, and to communicate threat intel in terms everyone reads the same way.

Do I need to memorise technique IDs?

No. You will absorb the ones in your area naturally. Understanding the tactic structure is what matters.
