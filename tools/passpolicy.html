Password Policy Checker

Test a password against a configurable length/complexity policy, rule by rule.

Password policies are where good intentions become weak passwords. Complexity requirements push everyone toward the same predictable shapes, and forced rotation makes people iterate a number on the end. This page lets you test a candidate against a policy and see what the policy is really buying you.

How to use it

  1. Enter a password to check.
  2. Set the minimum length and toggle the uppercase, digit and symbol requirements.
  3. See which rules pass and which fail.

What to take away

Modern guidance moved away from complexity

NIST SP 800-63B now recommends longer minimums, screening against known-breached passwords, and dropping composition rules and scheduled expiry — because both demonstrably make passwords worse.

Blocklists beat rules

Checking a candidate against known-breached passwords stops far more account takeovers than any arrangement of character-class requirements.

Rotation without cause is counterproductive

Forcing 90-day changes produces Summer2024! then Autumn2024!. Rotate on evidence of compromise instead.

Frequently asked questions

What does NIST actually recommend now?

A minimum of 8 characters and support for at least 64, screening against breach corpora, no composition rules, and no periodic expiry unless there is evidence of compromise.

Should I require a symbol?

It buys very little. Users satisfy it with a trailing ! that every cracking ruleset tries first. Length plus a breach blocklist is a far better use of the same friction.

Is my password sent anywhere?

No. The check runs entirely in your browser. Even so, test with a variation rather than a password you actually use.
