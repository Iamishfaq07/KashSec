Log Grep / Filter Playground

Filter a block of log lines with a regex — the exact "find the signal in the noise" skill IR work depends on.

Incident response is mostly reading logs faster than the attacker moved. This page gives you a pile of lines and a regex filter, which is the same loop as grep — narrow, read, narrow again — without needing a shell.

How to use it

  1. Paste log lines, one per line.
  2. Enter a case-insensitive regex filter.
  3. Refine until only the interesting lines remain.

What to take away

Subtract noise before adding signal

Filtering out what you know is routine usually reveals more, faster, than guessing what the attack looks like.

Time is the backbone of every investigation

Once you find one suspicious event, pivot to everything around that timestamp. The story lives in the neighbours.

Absence is evidence

A gap where logs should be, or a service that stopped logging mid-incident, is itself a finding — and often a deliberate one.

Frequently asked questions

What should I grep for first in auth logs?

"Failed password" and "Accepted password" together — a burst of failures followed by an acceptance is a successful brute force, and that pattern is the fastest way to spot one.

How is this different from grep?

It is the same idea in a browser, so you can practise the reasoning before you have a box to run commands on. Real work happens in grep, awk or a SIEM.
