STRIDE Threat-Model Worksheet

A guided Spoofing/Tampering/Repudiation/Info-Disclosure/DoS/Elevation worksheet, exportable as Markdown.

STRIDE is a checklist for asking "what could go wrong here" in a structured way: Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, and Elevation of privilege. Six prompts, applied to one component at a time, catch a surprising share of design flaws before any code exists.

How to use it

  1. Name the system or feature being modelled.
  2. Work through each STRIDE category for that component.
  3. Download the completed worksheet as Markdown.

What to take away

Model one component at a time

"Our whole platform" produces nothing useful. "The password reset endpoint" produces real findings in twenty minutes.

Each letter maps to a control

Spoofing to authentication, Tampering to integrity, Repudiation to logging, Information disclosure to confidentiality, DoS to availability, Elevation to authorisation. That mapping turns a threat into a task.

Design-time bugs are the expensive ones

A missing authorisation check in a design costs a conversation. The same gap found in production costs an incident.

Frequently asked questions

When should threat modelling happen?

While the design is still changeable — before or during implementation. Modelling after release still finds things, but the findings are much more expensive to act on.

Do I need a data flow diagram first?

A rough sketch of components, trust boundaries and data flows makes STRIDE far more productive. It does not need to be formal; a whiteboard photo works.

How does STRIDE compare to PASTA or attack trees?

STRIDE is the fastest to teach and the easiest to run in a normal team meeting. The others go deeper and cost more time; STRIDE is where nearly everyone should start.
