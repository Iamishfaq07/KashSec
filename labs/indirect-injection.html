Indirect Prompt Injection Challenge

You control a job posting an AI assistant is about to summarize for someone else. Plant an instruction it'll follow instead.

The dangerous form of prompt injection, because the victim is not the attacker. You control a job posting that an assistant is about to summarise for someone else — so you plant an instruction, and the model follows it on their behalf.

How to use it

  1. Write a posting that reads normally to a human.
  2. Embed an instruction aimed at the model.
  3. Watch the assistant act on it for the other user.

What to take away

Any retrieved content is an injection surface

Web pages, PDFs, emails, code comments, calendar invites — anything a model reads can carry instructions.

RAG widens the blast radius

A poisoned document in a shared knowledge base affects every user whose query retrieves it, not just the person who added it.

Frequently asked questions

Why is indirect injection worse than direct?

Because the victim never sees the payload and never consented. It is the difference between someone talking their own assistant into misbehaving and someone weaponising yours.

What actually reduces the risk?

Constrain what the model may do with retrieved content, keep privileged actions behind explicit authorisation, and require human confirmation before anything consequential.
