Webhook Signature Forgery Lab

Forge an HMAC-signed webhook without the secret, then flip on signature verification and watch it get rejected.

Forge an HMAC-signed webhook without knowing the secret, then switch verification on properly and watch the same request bounce. This is what a signature actually buys you, demonstrated in both directions.

How to use it

  1. Send a webhook with verification disabled.
  2. Observe what the receiver accepts.
  3. Enable verification and watch the forged request fail.

What to take away

Sign the raw body, verify before parsing

Computing the signature over a re-serialised object rather than the exact received bytes is a common and subtle break.

Compare signatures in constant time

A byte-by-byte early-exit comparison leaks the correct signature through timing. The Timing Attack Visualizer shows exactly how.

Include a timestamp

Without one, a captured valid request can be replayed forever. Reject signatures outside a short window.

Frequently asked questions

Why do webhooks need signatures at all?

The endpoint is public, so anyone can post to it. A signature is the only way to know the request came from the service you expect and was not modified in transit.

Is HTTPS enough on its own?

No. TLS protects the request in transit from third parties; it says nothing about who sent it. Anyone can open their own TLS connection to your endpoint.
