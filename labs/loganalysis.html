Log Analysis / IR Lab

A messy auth.log dump from a compromised box. Reconstruct the timeline and find the breach — blue-team skills, not exploitation.

A messy auth.log from a machine that was compromised. No exploitation here — this is the blue-team side: work out what happened, in what order, and where the attacker got in.

How to use it

  1. Scan for authentication failures and find the burst.
  2. Locate the success that follows it.
  3. Build the timeline forward from there — what did they do once inside?

What to take away

Failures then a success is the signature

Many failed passwords followed by an accepted one, from the same source, is a brute force that worked. It is the single most recognisable pattern in auth logs.

Pivot on time, then on identity

Find one confirmed event, examine everything around that timestamp, then follow the account or address wherever it goes.

Persistence is the next question

Attackers add keys, users, cron jobs and services. Finding entry without finding persistence means the incident is not over.

Frequently asked questions

Where do Linux authentication logs live?

/var/log/auth.log on Debian and Ubuntu, /var/log/secure on RHEL and derivatives. Systemd journals hold the same events via journalctl.

What is the first thing to check after a suspected compromise?

Who logged in, from where, and when — then what they ran. Preserve the evidence before changing anything, because remediation destroys the timeline.
