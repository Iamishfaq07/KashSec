DNS Recon Playground

A simulated dig. Query a target's likely subdomains and build a map of its real, sometimes-forgotten attack surface.

A simulated dig. Query a target's likely subdomains and assemble the map — including the staging box, the old admin panel and the forgotten service that nobody has patched since it stopped being important.

How to use it

  1. Query the obvious names.
  2. Work through common subdomain patterns.
  3. Assemble the picture of what is actually exposed.

What to take away

Forgotten hosts are the valuable ones

dev, staging, old and test frequently run outdated software with weaker controls and real data.

Record types answer different questions

A and AAAA give addresses, MX gives mail infrastructure, TXT often leaks vendor relationships through verification records, CNAME reveals third-party hosting.

Certificate transparency is a free subdomain list

Every issued certificate is logged publicly, so requesting one for a private hostname publishes its name.

Frequently asked questions

Is DNS enumeration legal?

Querying public DNS is public information lookup. Connecting to what you find is a separate question requiring authorisation.

How do defenders reduce DNS exposure?

Keep an authoritative inventory, remove records when services are decommissioned, avoid descriptive internal names in public zones, and monitor certificate transparency for your own domains.
