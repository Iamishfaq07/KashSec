Open Redirect Playground

A login page trusts a returnUrl parameter completely. Turn a genuinely trusted link into a post-login phishing weapon.

A login page that sends you wherever returnUrl says. On its own it is rated low severity — until you notice it turns a genuinely trusted domain into the first hop of a phishing link that passes every eyeball check.

How to use it

  1. Log in normally and watch the redirect.
  2. Change returnUrl to an external destination.
  3. See how the resulting link looks to a target.

What to take away

The value is the trusted domain

The link starts with a domain the victim knows. That is what defeats "check the link before clicking" advice.

It escalates OAuth bugs

A redirect chained into an OAuth flow can leak authorisation codes and tokens, turning a low-severity finding into account takeover.

Frequently asked questions

Why do triagers often rate open redirect as low?

In isolation it only moves a browser. It gets upgraded quickly when chained — into OAuth code theft, token leakage or a credible phishing campaign.

How is it fixed?

Allowlist permitted destinations, or accept only relative paths. Validating that the URL "contains" your domain fails against evil.com/?x=yoursite.com.
