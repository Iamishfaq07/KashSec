YARA Rule Builder Challenge

Three malware samples, one clean file. Write a YARA rule that matches every sample and nothing else.

Three malware samples and one clean file. Write a rule that catches all three and none of the clean one — which is the entire craft of detection engineering compressed into one exercise.

How to use it

  1. Find strings common to all three samples.
  2. Check none of them appear in the clean file.
  3. Tighten until you have full coverage and no false positive.

What to take away

The false positive is the hard constraint

A rule that catches everything is easy. A rule that catches everything and nothing else is detection engineering.

Choose durable strings

Pick what the author cannot casually change — protocol constants, unusual error text, a hardcoded mutex — not a filename.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a YARA rule production-ready?

Coverage of the family including variants, no benign matches, metadata identifying author and purpose, and testing against a large clean corpus before deployment.

How specific is too specific?

If a recompile evades it, it is too specific. Hashing a single sample is the extreme case — perfectly precise and useless against the next build.
